In Jonah Berger’s framework, **Social Currency** and **Triggers** work as complementary forces to drive both initial excitement and long-term viral momentum.

---

### 1. Motivation vs. Memory
* **Social Currency provides the motivation (Why people share):** People share remarkable, novel, or exclusive things because it makes them look smart, cool, or in-the-know. Social Currency generates **immediate word of mouth** right after someone experiences or hears about an idea.
* **Triggers provide the memory cue (When people share):** Triggers are environmental stimuli—sights, sounds, times of day, or daily habits—that prompt people to think about related ideas. Triggers drive **ongoing word of mouth** over weeks and months by keeping the idea top of mind.

---

### 2. Immediate Buzz vs. Ongoing Momentum
An idea needs both principles to achieve sustained virality:
* **High Social Currency without Triggers:** A trip to Disney World or a remarkable one-time event creates a massive initial spike of discussion, but because daily life offers few environmental reminders, conversations quickly fade.
* **Triggers without Social Currency:** Mundane products like Cheerios or Ziploc bags aren't inherently exciting to talk about, but because people encounter them constantly during breakfast or after meals, they generate high ongoing discussion.
* **Combining Both:** When an idea offers high Social Currency *and* is tied to a frequent environmental trigger, it gets both an explosive launch and sustained chatter. For example, Barclay Prime's **\$100 cheesesteak** carries immense Social Currency (it's remarkable and outrageous), while the constant presence of cheesesteaks in Philadelphia serves as a frequent trigger to bring it up in conversation.

---

### 3. The Rock Band Analogy
Berger compares their partnership to a music group:
> **Social Currency is the front man or woman**—exciting, fun, and getting lots of attention.
> **Triggers are the drummer or bassist**—the quiet workhorse laying down the steady rhythm that keeps the song moving.

Ultimately, the core rule connecting the two is: **"Social currency gets people talking, but Triggers keep them talking. Top of mind means tip of tongue."**.
